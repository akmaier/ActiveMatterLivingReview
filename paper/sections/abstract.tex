\noindent\textbf{Background:}
Robotic collectives permit experimental control of
microscopic interactions underlying active-matter
phenomena, but demonstrations of collective behavior
do not necessarily validate physical theories.

\noindent\textbf{Objectives:}
We mapped robotic platforms (O1), collective phenomena
(O2), quantitative theory comparisons (O3), control
and programmability (O4), and reproducibility (O5).

\noindent\textbf{Sources and eligibility:}
Searches covered multidisciplinary bibliographic
databases and preprint sources, supplemented by
citation-based and other discovery methods.
Eligible studies investigated at least two interacting,
actively driven artificial agents in physical hardware
and reported a quantitative collective or interaction
observable.

\noindent\textbf{Methods:}
We followed PRISMA 2020 \cite{Page2021prisma}.
A three-model LLM panel assisted screening;
structured extraction, evidence-based adjudication,
and a 200-record author-validation sample assessed
the workflow. Against final adjudicated labels,
panel sensitivity was 1.000, specificity 0.983,
and Cohen's $\kappa=0.922$; agreement with the
initial blind labels was substantially lower.

\noindent\textbf{Results:}
Of 15\,034 identified database records,
6\,335 duplicates were removed.
The October 17, 2026 evidence base
contains 489 studies.
Wheeled or legged robots were the largest platform
class (203 studies), followed by other platforms
(87), microscopic robotic agents (76), and
vibration-driven robots (63).
Collective motion, transport, clustering,
and self-assembly were widely represented.
Quantitative model comparisons were less
common than qualitative demonstrations.
In the corrected 489-study extraction,
81 studies received low-risk ratings for
quantitative theory comparison and 38 for
data and code availability.

\noindent\textbf{Limitations:}
Experimental heterogeneity, incomplete replication
and finite-size reporting, abstract-only assessments,
and LLM-assisted eligibility and extraction limit
certainty. The merged duplicate requires final
reconciliation of extraction-derived totals.

\noindent\textbf{Conclusions:}
Robots provide versatile experimental realizations
of active matter. Stronger theoretical conclusions
require independently constrained predictions,
replication, and systematic parameter variation.
Monthly living updates will reassess the evidence.
